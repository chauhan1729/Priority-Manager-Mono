# Karmic Management — the whole book, in plain words
*A short, practical guide to the Eight Rules of Karmic Management. Read one rule a day, try the "Try this," and watch what comes back.*

> **The promise:** stop stressing over whether things will work out. When you make the right people successful first, your own success stops being a gamble and starts being something you can count on.

The one idea behind everything: **what goes around comes around.** Every kind thing you do, say, or even think plants a seed in your mind. Later that seed grows and shapes what happens to you. So the surest way to get something is to first help someone else get the same thing.

---

## The big idea (in three lines)
1. Nothing "just happens" to you at random — it grows from seeds you planted by how you treated others.
2. You plant good seeds mainly through other people: help them win, and you've planted a win for yourself.
3. Do this on purpose, every day, and your results stop being luck and start being sure.

**Example:** You want more clients. Instead of only chasing them, you spend ten minutes helping a peer land *their* client — freely, expecting nothing. That's a seed for your own clients to show up.

---

## Rule 1 — Stop doing things that don't work
**The idea:** If something only works *sometimes*, it isn't really the thing that's working — you're just playing the odds. Be honest about what actually delivers and what only *looks* busy.

**Try this:**
- Write down five things you're counting on this week, and the honest odds each works.
- Notice how much stress those "maybes" cause.
- Circle the one you keep repeating even though it keeps flopping — and stop.

**Example:** You cold-call the same list every quarter and it lands maybe once in ten. That's not a strategy, that's a coin toss. Admit it isn't working, and free up that energy for something that plants real seeds.

---

## Rule 2 — Look for the cause behind the cause
**The idea:** The obvious cause usually isn't the real one. The key turns the car, but the *battery* is what really starts it. Behind your results sits a hidden cause: the seeds from how you've treated people. That's the "battery."

**Try this:**
- When something goes right, don't stop at "the call worked." Ask: *what kindness of mine might have set this up?*
- When something goes wrong, look under the hood for the real cause instead of banging on the key.

**Example:** A deal closes and everyone says "great pitch." The pitch is the key. The battery was the month you spent quietly making your teammates look good — that's what really made the deal land.

---

## Rule 3 — Pick your four karmic partners *(the heart of it)*
**The idea:** You can't plant seeds alone — you need people to bounce them off, like an echo needs a wall. There are four groups to make successful, and your job is to genuinely help each one win:
- **Co-workers** — the people you work with.
- **Customers** — whoever the work is *for*.
- **Suppliers** — the people who make your work possible.
- **The World** — someone beyond your own circle.

**The First Law of Karma:** *whatever you want from life, you must first do for someone else.*

**Try this:**
- Name **one real person** in each group.
- For each, write **what their success would actually look like** — specific, not vague.
- No strings attached — you're simply trying to make them win.

**Example:** Co-worker = Priya, success = "ships her first solo project." Customer = Acme, success = "renews and refers a friend." Supplier = your printer, success = "gets paid on time and a five-star review." World = the shelter down the street, success = "20 meals funded."

**In this app:** this is the **Karmic Partners** tab — name your four (or a few per group) and their success vision.

---

## Rule 4 — Start from yourself (and keep a clear mind)
**The idea:** Nothing moves until *you* move first — you make the first kind gesture, unilaterally. And because this way of thinking is easy to lose the moment you're tired or rattled, you have to keep yourself clear. The book's **Seven-Point Program for a clear mind:** yoga, meditation, a personal ethical code, keep learning, serve others daily, eat well, and rest well.

**Your personal ethical code** — the part that most shapes the thoughts you hear all day:
- **Protect life** — don't harm anyone; avoid even hurtful words and thoughts.
- **Respect others' things** — never steal or fudge (no "small" cheating).
- **Respect others' relationships.**
- **Be truthful** — never leave someone with an impression you know is false.
- **Stay clear** — don't get pulled into things that fog your mind.

**Try this:** keep the code simple, keep it daily, and each night ask honestly: did I keep it, or slip? No guilt — just an honest note.

**Example:** You're tempted to tell a customer their order is "almost ready" when it hasn't started. That small lie plants a seed for your own plans to stall. Tell the simple truth instead.

**In this app:** the **Ethics Code** tab (edit your code + nightly kept/slipped check) and the **Daily Log** (your best and worst of the day).

---

## Rule 5 — Stop stressing over decisions
**The idea:** Life feels heavy because we treat every choice as a gamble. But if you've planted the seeds, the outcome is already handled — the decision matters far less than you think. You can put down the constant "will this work?" worry.

**Try this:**
- Before agonizing over option A vs. B, ask: *have I done my seed-work — did I help my partners today?*
- If yes, pick the one your gut likes and move. Both will tend to work.

**Example:** You're stuck choosing between two ad ideas. If the seeds are planted, either will do fine — so stop losing a day to it, trust your instinct, and go.

---

## Rule 6 — "Load your stapler" (do the one small thing)
**The idea:** A stapler works if it's *loaded* — the color doesn't matter. Your actions are the same: a "loaded" action (one that helps a partner) is sure to work. So instead of deciding between staplers, just make sure yours is loaded.

**Try this — the core daily habit:**
- Each morning, write **one small, doable action** for each of your four partners — something you can honestly finish today.
- Keep it modest. Not "transform the team" — just "thank Sam for the late night."
- Do them. That's loading your stapler.

**Example:** Co-worker → "review Priya's PR before lunch." Customer → "send Acme the cheaper option." Supplier → "pay the invoice today." World → "donate this week's overflow." Four small seeds, every day.

**In this app:** each partner card's **"What will you do to make them successful?"** line — that's exactly this rule.

---

## Rule 7 — Ride your problems over the top
**The idea:** Old seeds you planted long ago will still pop up as problems — that's normal, and problems are actually *useful*: each one shows you exactly which seed to shut off. **Two more laws:** *karma always grows* (a tiny seed becomes a big result), and *if something you don't like is happening to you, stop doing it to others.*

**Try this — the karmic shut-off valve:**
- When a problem shows up, don't just fight the surface (don't "fire Frank").
- Ask: *what kind of thing must I have done to plant this?* (A complainer on your team? You must have complained somewhere.)
- Then stop doing that thing completely, starting now. That starves the seed.

**Example:** A teammate won't stop grumbling. Reacting with your own grumbling only plants more of it. Instead, go absolutely quiet on complaints yourself — and watch the grumbling fade, without a confrontation.

---

## Rule 8 — Re-invest the good
**The idea:** Seeds wear out as they give their fruit — success fades unless you replant. So the moment something goes well, put some of it back into your partners. Like a farmer saving 10% of the harvest for next year's seed.

**Try this:**
- After a win, spend a slice of it on the people who helped — a thank-you, credit, a treat, a gift.
- Celebrate together, out loud, with real gratitude. That celebration is itself the most powerful seed.

**Example:** A project pays off. You take the team to dinner, write your boss a note naming each person's strength, thank your supplier, and donate a little to someone outside. That's next year's harvest, planted today.
