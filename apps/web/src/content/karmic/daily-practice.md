# Daily Practice — Karmic Seeds for Living
*Read this each morning (a few minutes). Log once at night. The full "why" is in [karmic-imprint.md](karmic-imprint.md) — read that once a week.*

> **The idea, in three lines:**
> 1. Nothing is good or bad on its own. If it were, everyone would see it the same way — but they don't.
> 2. What you see is the fruit of seeds you planted by how you treated others — in what you do, say, and (most of all) think.
> 3. Plant good seeds, know why you're doing it, and you shape your own future. Bad seeds can't grow good fruit.

**Morning cue:** Before the day starts, sit quietly for a few minutes. Take 10 slow breaths. Then pick one problem, remember it isn't fixed, trace it back to your own past seed, and picture yourself handling it the right way today. End by picturing the person you want to become. *(Full how-to: weekly read, Part 8.)*

## The Seven — read all, every day

### ① Wealth & Generosity
**The idea:** Money grows from a giving heart, not just hard work. When you keep a warm "I want others to win too" feeling all day — and share what you have, even a little — you plant a seed for your own money to grow. *(DC #1, #9)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Find one chance to give — money, help, credit, time, or a tip.
2. Give it freely, expecting nothing back. Feel good as you give — that feeling makes the seed strong.
3. If a selfish "why should I?" thought pops up, notice it and let it go.

**Example:** A coworker asks how you won a client. The stingy voice says "keep it to yourself." Instead, you take five minutes and tell them everything, hoping they win too. It costs you nothing — and you just planted a seed for your own wealth.

**Avoid:** being stingy, cutting corners, envy of others' wins, earning money the wrong way. *(DC #1–2; Wheel v37, v60)*

**Log tonight:** Did you give, or did the stingy voice win? Write the exact moment — e.g. *"11:20 — showed Rahul my full client list."*

### ② Focus & Clarity
**The idea:** A sharp, calm mind is a seed too. You grow it by giving your mind quiet time on the big questions of life, and by not wasting it on idle talk and empty plans. *(DC #11, #12, #17)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Before your hardest task, take 10 slow breaths to settle.
2. Once today, sit quietly and ask a real question: Are my priorities right? What matters most?
3. Finish one thing you started before jumping to the next.

**Example:** You can't focus in a big meeting. The fix isn't more coffee — it's the few quiet minutes you spent this morning putting your mind on what really matters. That calm plants the seed for a clear mind when you need it.

**Avoid:** idle chatter, gossip, news you can't act on, and grand plans you won't follow through. *(DC #37–38; Wheel v30)*

**Log tonight:** One moment you were focused, and one time your mind scattered.

### ③ Discipline & Integrity
**The idea:** Being fully honest — in every word, and being exactly who you seem to be — plants a clear mind and an honest world around you. Small lies plant the opposite. *(DC #8, #10, #25)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Make your words match what you really see. Leave people with the true picture, not a shaded one.
2. Keep every promise you make today, even tiny ones.
3. Catch one place where you'd normally stretch the truth a little — and don't.

**Example:** You're tempted to tell a customer their order is "almost ready" when it hasn't even started. That small lie plants the seed for your own plans to stop working and for people to doubt you. Tell the simple truth instead.

**Avoid:** small lies, exaggerations, fudging numbers, or trying to win something good by doing something wrong. *(DC #45–46; Wheel v27, v31)*

**Log tonight:** One honest moment, and one time you stretched the truth.

### ④ Relationships & Speech
**The idea:** Bring people together and speak with care, and your world fills with harmony. Divide people or speak harshly, and your world fills with conflict. *(DC #6, #42)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Say only what has a purpose — and say it kindly.
2. Bring two people a little closer, even in a small way.
3. Catch one useless or unkind sentence before it leaves your mouth.

**Example:** You're about to say "Did you hear what she said about you?" That one sentence plants the seed for your own team to fight and partners to turn on you. Stay quiet, or say something that brings them together.

**Avoid:** gossip, talk that splits people apart, harsh words (blunt or sweetly cutting), and empty chatter. *(DC #15, #37; Wheel v14, v16)*

**Log tonight:** One time you brought people together, and one sharp or useless thing you said.

### ⑤ Health & Anger/Peace
**The idea:** Care for others' health and comfort, and stay free of anger — that plants your own good health and calm. Anger plants illness, aging, and harsh treatment. *(DC #19, #22, #29)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Notice one thing that would normally upset you — and step back before it turns to anger.
2. Ease one person's load or discomfort.
3. Catch irritation early, before it grows.

**Example:** Traffic is bad and you feel anger rising. Letting it go isn't just "being nice" — you're refusing to plant a seed that harms your own health and looks. Stepping back keeps you well.

**Avoid:** anger, getting rattled, wishing trouble on anyone, or harming others. *(DC #19, #28; Wheel v10, v38)*

**Log tonight:** One time you stayed calm, and one time anger won.

### ⑥ Leadership, Respect & Gratitude
**The idea:** Stay humble, listen to everyone, give others credit, and feel grateful — and you grow into a respected leader people trust. Pride and ingratitude do the reverse. *(DC #3, #30, #32, #36)*

**How to do it today:**
1. Truly listen to someone, whatever their rank.
2. Give real credit to someone for something they did well.
3. Thank one person who quietly helps you.

**Example:** You did most of the work, so you want the credit. But grabbing it plants the seed for people to mislead and ignore you. Passing credit to your team plants respect and trust — and you lose nothing real.

**Avoid:** pride, needing praise, ingratitude, and quiet pleasure at others' failures. *(DC #3, #16, #36; Wheel v43, v61)*

**Log tonight:** One time you gave credit or thanks, and one time pride got in the way.

### ⑦ Adversity & Ego
**The idea:** When trouble hits, it's your own past seed coming back — not bad luck, and not someone else's fault. Take it as your own, wish others well, and the cycle ends. The real enemy is always "me first." *(Wheel v10–48)*

**How to do it today:**
1. When something goes wrong, say: "This is my own seed returning. From now, let me do better."
2. Breathe in someone else's trouble; breathe out your calm and good wishes (this is *tonglen*).
3. Take on one person's burden instead of adding to it.

**Example:** A deal falls through and you want to blame a colleague. Instead you think, "This came from my own past seed," and go help someone else with their problem. Blaming plants more trouble; taking responsibility ends it.

**Avoid:** blaming others, self-pity, and defending "me" above everyone else. *(Wheel v39, v49–53)*

**Log tonight:** One problem you took as your own seed, and one time you blamed others or pitied yourself.

## Evening — the Act of Truth
On a day you were honest and kind in what you did, said, and thought, say this — and picture warm golden light flowing from your heart to everyone near you, then everyone heading home, then everyone waiting for them:

> *If it's true that today I was honest and kind in all I did, said, and even thought toward others — then may a new power be born. And by it, may I and everyone in my world find real happiness and success, together.*

---

## The Log — once, each night (about 2 minutes)
Pick your **top 1–3 aspects** for now. For each, write:
- **➕** one thing you did well today — be exact (place, time, who). Not "I was kind," but "3:15 — thanked Susan at her desk."
- **➖** one slip on the same aspect.
- **✔** one small, easy thing to try tomorrow.

Then write the day's **best three** and **worst three** thoughts, words, or deeds.

**Remember:** Be specific — general notes don't work. No guilt — only a calm "next time I'll do it differently." You're planting seeds and watching them grow. By tracking, you change; by changing, your world changes.
