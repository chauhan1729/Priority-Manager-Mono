# Karmic Imprints — The Full Guide (Weekly Read)
### From *The Diamond Cutter* (Geshe Michael Roach) and *The Wheel of Sharp Weapons* (Dharmarakshita)

## PART 1 — HOW IT ALL WORKS (the foundation)

Everything in both books rests on four simple ideas. Get these first — the rest only works once you see *why*.

### 1. Nothing is good or bad on its own
A person, a deal, a rainy day — none of them is good or bad by itself. How do we know? Because if a thing were *truly* irritating, **everyone** would find it irritating. But they never do. The coworker who drives you crazy is someone else's dear friend. The rain that ruins your day makes the umbrella seller's day. So the "good" or "bad" is **not inside the thing** — the thing is blank, open, neutral. This blankness is its hidden potential: it can become anything.

**Example:** You buy a building, and prices drop — bad, for you. But it's good for the movers you hired, good for staff who now have more space, good for the neighbors. Same building, opposite meanings. So the building itself isn't "a bad deal." The bad-ness came from somewhere else — from you.

### 2. What you see comes from you
If the good or bad isn't in the thing, and it can't come from nowhere, then it comes from **your own mind**. Not by wishing — you can't wish a loss away. Something *forces* you to see things the way you do. That something is a **seed** (an "imprint") planted in your mind earlier. This is the real meaning of *karma*.

### 3. How seeds are planted
Every time you **do**, **say**, or even **think** something toward another person, your mind records it — like a camera that never turns off. Help someone → a good seed. Hurt someone → a bad seed. And **thought is the strongest of the three** — your deepest seeds come from your attitudes alone.

**Six things make a seed stronger (good or bad):**
1. **Why you did it** — a caring reason plants a stronger seed than a selfish one.
2. **How strong the feeling** — acting in hot anger plants a deeper seed.
3. **Knowing what you're doing** — on purpose plants more than by accident.
4. **Who it's toward** — someone in real need, someone who's been very kind to you, or a person of great character → a much stronger seed.
5. **The way you do it** — the style (e.g. paying late *and* being rude about it).
6. **How you feel after** — glad you did it? would you do it again? owning it makes the seed stronger.

### 4. How seeds grow (four rules)
1. **Like grows like** — a bad act can only grow into a bad experience; a good act only into a good one. (Grapes never grow from thorns.)
2. **Seeds grow big over time** — a tiny act, left in the mind, swells into a huge experience — like an acorn becoming a ton of oak.
3. **Nothing reaches you without a seed** — every person, event, and even your own mood was first caused by a seed of yours.
4. **No seed is wasted** — once planted, it *must* grow into an experience.

**Example of how seeds grow:** A few small white lies to close deals, told over months, feel like nothing at the time. But each is a seed. Left in the mind they swell — and one day they surface as a whole reality: your trusted strategies stop working, and people stop believing you even when you're honest. A big problem grown from tiny seeds.

**The master key:** Even a small act, done while you *understand* how seeds work, plants a powerful seed. Knowing why you're doing it multiplies the effect.

**One comfort:** the trouble you have *now* was usually planted long ago, and may not match how you act today. That's fine. The fix is always the same — stop planting that seed, even in tiny ways, and plant its opposite.

## PART 2 — THE SEVEN MASTER SEEDS
The oldest short list of seeds and what they grow (from the master Nagarjuna). All 46 solutions in Part 3 are really just these seven, spelled out for specific problems — so if you understand these seven, you understand the whole system.

| To grow this | Plant this seed |
|---|---|
| Wealth | **Giving** — a generous mind |
| A happy world around you | **Ethics** — a clean, kind life |
| Health & good looks | **Patience** — never getting angry |
| Leadership & respect | **Effort** — joy in helping and building |
| A focused mind | **Concentration** — quiet, steady practice |
| Freedom from a world that won't cooperate | **Wisdom** — learning how seeds and emptiness work |
| Getting all you (and others) wish for | **Compassion** — real care for everyone |

Here is each one in plain words:

**1. Giving → Wealth.** Giving is simply sharing what you have — money, time, help, even credit. It is the master seed for money. Remember, it's the generous *feeling* that plants wealth, not the size of the gift; a small gift given warmly beats a large one given grudgingly.
*Example:* quietly sharing a useful lead with a coworker, with a real wish for them to win too, plants a seed for your own prosperity.

**2. Ethics → A happy world.** Ethics just means doing good and avoiding harm to others. A clean life plants a world that feels good to live in — pleasant surroundings and circumstances that tend to go your way.
*Example:* keeping all your dealings honest and hurting no one plants a life where things around you quietly work in your favor, without you forcing them.

**3. Patience → Health & good looks.** Patience means letting go of anger — not bottling it up, but not letting it rise in the first place. Anger burns your health and ages you; patience keeps you well and young-looking.
*Example:* when someone snaps at you and you stay calm instead of snapping back, you're planting good health and a fresh face for years to come.

**4. Effort → Leadership & respect.** The "effort" here is *joy* in doing good, helpful things — not grim overwork. Delighting in building and helping plants a natural place as someone people look up to.
*Example:* cheerfully jumping in to help a struggling teammate plants the seed for others to follow you and value what you say.

**5. Concentration → A focused mind.** Concentration is quiet, steady practice that calms and steadies the mind — like the morning Silent Time (Part 7). It plants the power to focus when it really counts.
*Example:* a few quiet minutes each morning plant the sharp, clear focus you'll feel later in a high-pressure meeting.

**6. Wisdom → Freedom.** Wisdom means understanding how things really work — that nothing is fixed, and everything you meet comes from your own seeds. This frees you from a world that won't cooperate, because now you can change it at the root instead of fighting it.
*Example:* seeing that a "bad market" is really your own seed, not a fixed fact, frees you to stop panicking and plant a better one.

**7. Compassion → Everything you (and others) wish for.** Compassion is real care for everyone, joined with the wish to end their suffering. It's the strongest seed of all, and it brings about every good thing — for you and for others, together.
*Example:* genuinely wishing a competitor well plants the seed for everyone, yourself included, to thrive — there turns out to be more than enough to go around.

## PART 3 — THE 46 PROBLEMS & THEIR FIXES (Diamond Cutter)
Each one is a real problem, the hidden seed that causes it, and how to plant the opposite seed. Pick the 2–3 that trouble you most for your nightly log. Remember: you fix the world by fixing yourself, not by trying to change the people around you.

### Money, position, enjoyment

**1. Your money is shaky and up-and-down.**
*Cause:* you've been unwilling to share your profit, or you've earned in ways that aren't clean. *Fix:* share what you make with the people who helped you make it, and never take a wrong penny. The amount doesn't matter — the willingness does.
*Example:* a bonus lands. Instead of quietly pocketing it all, you share a little with the team that earned it with you. Small seed, steady money.

**2. Your tools and equipment keep going outdated or breaking.**
*Cause:* envying other people's businesses. *Fix:* stop comparing; make your own work creative and fun, and don't resent others' success.

**3. You're losing your authority.**
*Cause:* acting arrogant, above people. *Fix:* come down to their level, sit with them, and listen.
*Example:* instead of handing down orders from your office, you spend ten minutes at a junior person's desk actually hearing them out.

**4. You can't enjoy what you've earned.**
*Cause:* begrudging others their success and constantly comparing yourself. *Fix:* stop comparing; simply enjoy what you have.

**5. Nothing ever feels like enough.**
Same seed and fix as #4.

**6. The people around you are always fighting.**
*Cause:* talk that pushes people apart — even true talk, if the aim is to divide. *Fix:* stop divisive talk completely. Note the fix isn't to lecture *them* to get along — it's to change what you say.
*Example:* you catch yourself about to say "You know what he really thinks of you?" — and you don't. Over time the fighting around you fades.

**7. You keep falling out with partners.**
Same seed and fix as #6.

**8. You second-guess yourself; you can't decide.**
*Cause:* two things — not caring for your people, and pretending to be something you're not. *Fix:* care for your team, and be exactly who you are. Honesty makes your mind clear and your decisions crisp.

**9. You can't raise cash for a good opportunity.**
*Cause:* being a cheapskate. *Fix:* give generously and make deals win-win, all day long. It's the flowing, generous mind that plants the money.
*Example:* like Ben Franklin, who met competition by inviting his rivals to build a Chamber of Commerce — growing the whole market so everyone won.

**10. "Acts of God" — bad weather, outages, traffic — keep hurting you.**
*Cause:* breaking your promises, especially the standards you set for yourself. *Fix:* keep your word, above all to your own principles.

### Mind, understanding, focus

**11. You can't concentrate when the pressure is on.**
*Cause:* never giving your mind quiet time on the big questions of life. *Fix:* each day, sit calmly with the real questions — Are my priorities right? What matters? That quiet plants a fierce ability to focus.

**12. You can't grasp big systems or market patterns.**
*Cause:* dodging the deepest question of *why* the world works the way it does. *Fix:* honestly reflect on it now and then. You can't understand your business if you won't face how anything works.

**13. Rents are too high; you can't find space.**
*Cause:* not helping others find a place when they needed one. *Fix:* actively help others find room.
*Example:* it sounds far-fetched that turning away a relative who needed a bed could block your branch office from finding a home — but that's the rule. Help people find space, and space opens for you.

**14. Impressive, capable people won't connect with you.**
*Cause:* choosing partners for money instead of character. *Fix:* choose people of real integrity, even when it costs you short-term.

**15. The competition is ruthless and always wins.**
*Cause:* harsh speech. That includes both blunt insults *and* sweet words meant to wound (e.g. "Loved your presentation!" to someone who just failed). *Fix:* drop both kinds of rough talk, and the competition softens.

**16. People get close to you, then stab you in the back.**
*Cause:* quiet, private pleasure when others have trouble. *Fix:* feel real sympathy for anyone struggling, even a rival.

**17. You plan a project carefully, work hard, and it just flops.**
*Cause:* a wrong idea of how things really work — like thinking "enough hours = success." *Fix:* act from the right understanding (these principles). It's not capital, people, market, or hours that decide it — it's the seeds. Thinking straight plants more straight thinking.

**18. Nobody steps up to help when you're in a crunch.**
*Cause:* taking pleasure in others' problems. *Fix:* jump in to help wherever you can — even offering an aspirin to someone with a headache — and refuse the morbid interest in others' woes.

**19. You can't control your temper.**
*Cause:* wishing trouble on others, or at least not minding when they have it. *Fix:* wish ill on no one. Trouble is a common enemy to us all.

**20. The market swings wildly, with no sense to it.**
*Cause:* wishing others would fail — a chaotic wish. *Fix:* wish everyone well, even competitors. That plants a steady, growing market.

**21. Corruption surrounds you.**
*Cause:* jealousy of others' happiness. *Fix:* take real joy in everyone's wins — big or small, yours or a rival's.

### Health, appearance, mood

**22. Small health problems keep cropping up over your career.**
*Cause:* not caring for others' health and comfort. *Fix:* walk through your world and fix what's hurting people — bad lighting, unsafe setups, overwork. Caring for others' health plants your own.

**23. Strategies that used to work stop working.**
*Cause:* some dishonesty in how you earn — even small. *Fix:* remove the tiniest stretch of your integrity, and the old approach works again.

**24. You get low moods and self-doubt.**
*Cause:* pushing your people to be less than fully honest. *Fix:* support total honesty in everyone who works for you. Joy in your work comes from that.

**29. The years are aging your looks.**
*Cause:* anger. *Fix:* don't just avoid anger — avoid its *prelude*, getting rattled or upset over a bad order or a traffic jam. Steady calm keeps you looking young.

### Speech & recognition

**25. People don't believe you, even when you tell the truth.**
*Cause:* past lies, even small ones. *Fix:* be strictly accurate — leave people with the exact picture you hold in your own mind. This is harder than ordinary "honesty," but it plants deep trust.

**26. Team efforts and partnerships won't come together.**
*Cause:* dishonesty. *Fix:* total honesty; make sure others walk away with the same understanding you have.

**27. You work in an industry where everyone cheats.**
*Cause:* your own past dishonesty. *Fix:* be completely straight, and slowly you'll meet fewer and fewer people who cheat — because each cheater is your own seed appearing.

**28. Your boss speaks to you insultingly.**
*Cause:* your own anger — answering an insult with an insult replants the very seed. *Fix:* stop the cycle from your side. Refuse to return insult for insult, and over time these people leave your life.

**30. However well you do, people criticize you.**
*Cause:* ignoring how your words and acts land on others. *Fix:* before you speak or act, weigh how it affects the people around you.

**37. No one respects your suggestions.**
*Cause:* useless talk — gossip, endless chatter about news you can't affect. *Fix:* speak only what has purpose.
*Example:* you make a great suggestion in a meeting and it's ignored, then the boss says the same thing and it's brilliant. The fix isn't a louder voice — it's cutting the idle talk that planted the seed.

**38. You've lost your confidence.**
*Cause:* empty talk — big plans and announcements you never follow through on. *Fix:* speak only of what you truly intend to do.

**39. You can't rest or enjoy time off.**
*Cause:* purposeless talk. *Fix:* speak only when it means something. The reward is a settled, content feeling.

**40. Your timing is always off.**
*Cause:* talking of things you never really intend to carry through. *Fix:* do what you say you'll do.

**41. No one listens when you ask them to do something.**
*Cause:* always talking about unimportant things. *Fix:* think before you speak, and say only what truly helps.

### Reliability, cooperation, environment

**31. Work you hand off never gets done.**
*Cause:* not helping other people's work. *Fix:* be their champion — lend your *best* people when another team needs help, even at your own cost. Soon your handed-off work comes back early and excellent.

**32. Projects start strong, then go sour.**
*Cause:* ingratitude. *Fix:* practice gratitude — really notice the many people (paid or not) who spend hours of their short lives helping you, and thank them constantly.
*Example:* looking down at your breakfast, remember the hundreds of hands that brought it to your table. Happy people feel this; unhappy people avoid it.

**33. You keep ending up in filthy, polluted places.**
*Cause:* lewdness and sexual misconduct around you. *Fix:* clean out harassment and lewd talk in your world. Odd as it sounds, cleaning that up makes your surroundings physically nicer too.

**34. People are unreliable; you have to triple-check everything.**
*Cause:* being unreliable in your own home and family. *Fix:* be someone your spouse and children can count on, and reliable people appear around you.

**35. You have no independence; you can't decide alone.**
*Cause:* not respecting others' property and space. *Fix:* share your resources freely with the team. Think "one body" — what's good for another part is good for you too.

**36. Customers, suppliers, and staff mislead you.**
*Cause:* pride, and living for others' recognition. *Fix:* stay humble and listen to everyone; and hunt for chances to give recognition to others instead of craving it for yourself.

**42. People in your company fight a lot.**
*Cause:* dividing talk, plus quiet pleasure at others' trouble. *Fix:* bring people together in small ways all day, and refuse malicious thoughts. (Notice: almost every bad seed makes you see the very thing you were trying to cause — the wheel turns itself.)

### The deepest problems — how you see the world

**43. Integrity just isn't respected where you work ("nice guys finish last").**
*Cause:* your own seeds, not the industry. *Fix:* don't run to a "better" industry — the same mix of people follows you everywhere. Train yourself hard in integrity, and your own world changes.
*Example:* an employee quits, sure their coworker is the problem. Six months later, at a new company, they're making the exact same complaints. The problem was never the place.

**44. You're losing your business touch — slower, duller than before.**
*Cause:* failing to live by, or even notice, the law that good brings good. *Fix:* understand how seeds work and follow integrity — this literally sharpens your mind.

**45. Justice fails you — you're wronged, but the boss or courts won't help.**
*Cause:* trying to get a good result from a harmful act — small lies to close a deal, cheating on taxes. *Fix:* deeply understand that good can never come from bad. Every time you act on the opposite belief, you plant the seed for justice to turn against you.

**46. Your own integrity has slipped over the years.**
*Cause:* long holding the quiet belief that integrity doesn't matter in business. *Fix:* work hard to overcome that inherited belief; re-examine the ideas you grew up with, and study these principles again and again. (Many of our beliefs about success came from first-grade teachers — worth a fresh look as an adult.)

## PART 4 — THE WHEEL OF SHARP WEAPONS (the same law, sharper)
Dharmarakshita's picture: **whatever suffering hits you is your own weapon returning** — like a blacksmith killed by the very sword he forged, or an arrow-maker killed by his own arrow. Each pain below gets three things: the **cause** (what you once did to plant it), the **resolve** (what to do instead now), and an **example** to make it real. After sitting with each, do the Giving-and-Taking practice (Part 7) and firmly decide to change. The healthy response to any pain is never "why me?" — it's "this is my own arrow returning; from now, let me not fire such arrows."

### Pains of the body and health

**Body pain and disease (v10).**
- *Cause:* In this life and before, you have harmed other beings' bodies — beaten them, caged them, or hurt them. That weapon now swings back as pain in your own body.
- *Resolve:* Wish to take others' sickness onto yourself, and from now on protect and care for people's bodies.
- *Example:* When illness strikes, don't only resent it. Sit with it and think, "May this pain of mine stand in for the pain of everyone who is sick right now" — turning the hurt into a seed of compassion instead of a fresh seed of anger.

**Depression and mental anguish (v11).**
- *Cause:* You have disturbed the peace of others' minds — upset your parents, teachers, coworkers, or friends. That disturbance returns as your own anxious, heavy mind.
- *Resolve:* Take others' mental pain onto yourself, and stop unsettling people. Never blame your bad moods on others — see them as your own returning seed.
- *Example:* When gloom lands for no clear reason, instead of hunting for who "made" you feel this way, picture everyone weighed down by worry and breathe their heaviness in, sending them your calm.

**Sudden illness, gout, swelling (v26).**
- *Cause:* You recklessly used or enjoyed what had been offered or entrusted to others — especially shared or sacred things — treating it as your own.
- *Resolve:* Never take for yourself what belongs to others or was given for a shared purpose; treat what's entrusted to you as untouchable.
- *Example:* The book notes how quickly candles offered at a holy place get snatched away the moment backs are turned. Don't be that hand — guard what others have given, never skim from it.

**Sudden strokes and fainting (v27).**
- *Cause:* You broke solemn promises and pledges — especially vows made to a teacher or guide. A broken word of honor returns as a sudden collapse in the body.
- *Resolve:* Keep your pledges, and drop the actions that lead you to break them.
- *Example:* You promise a mentor you'll follow through on something, then quietly let it slide. That broken pledge is a seed. Keep even the private promises you make to those who guide you.

**Crippling illness, or harm rising against you (v34).**
- *Cause:* You let your commitments decay and belittled the practices meant to protect the mind, underestimating their power.
- *Resolve:* Drop narrow, one-sided views, and honor what you've committed to.
- *Example:* Waving off your own disciplines as "not really important" plants the seed for illness and outside harm to rise against you. Take your commitments seriously and they protect you.

**Being ugly or mocked (v38).**
- *Cause:* You made ugliness through anger — ugly scenes, harsh words — and disrespected or mocked beautiful and sacred things.
- *Resolve:* Make beautiful things, appreciate others' work, and stay warm and good-cheered rather than short-tempered.
- *Example:* The book tells of Rechungpa, so handsome that a woman who stormed out to scold him softened instantly and made offerings instead — the fruit of past respect for beauty. Anger disfigures your future face; cheer beautifies it.

### Money and possessions

**Hunger, thirst, and poverty (v12).**
- *Cause:* You hoarded what you had, refused to share, and even took or grabbed from others. Miserliness is the very seed of want.
- *Resolve:* Give freely. As Tsong Khapa said, "Miserliness is the way to become poor, not rich; generosity is the way to become rich, not poor."
- *Example:* The exact moment you feel lack and want to clutch tighter is the moment to give a little anyway — that reversed reflex plants the seed for plenty.

**Your things break, get lost, or spoil (v19).**
- *Cause:* You were careless with other people's belongings, and wished good things for yourself alone.
- *Resolve:* Treat others' property with care, and wish to give people what they need.
- *Example:* Borrowing a tool and returning it damaged — or shrugging when you misuse what's shared — seeds a life where your own things keep failing you. Handle others' things better than your own.

**Poor no matter how hard you try (v37).**
- *Cause:* You were a miser — you gave little to those in need and made only stingy offerings.
- *Resolve:* Give openly to those in need. As Atisa said, "To save our wealth is useless, for it cannot follow us through death; to give it away now is a treasure for the future."
- *Example:* The book tells of a poor couple who shared a single robe. When the old woman offered even that one shabby robe with a full heart, the Buddha honored it greatly and carried it in his golden hand. It's the open heart, not the size of the gift, that plants wealth.

### Freedom and circumstances

**No freedom — you must obey others (v13).**
- *Cause:* You looked down on those below you and used them as servants for your own ends.
- *Resolve:* Humbly put yourself in the service of others. As Shantideva said, having given his body to others, it was theirs to use as they wished.
- *Example:* Ordering people around as if their time were worth less than yours seeds a life where you have no say over your own. Serve down the ladder, not only up it.

**Wretched, unpleasant surroundings (v15).**
- *Cause:* You held a negative outlook and saw only people's flaws, never able to hold a clean, pure view.
- *Resolve:* Look for the good, and train yourself to see your surroundings as pure.
- *Example:* The book advises: if you have an enemy, see them as sent to help burn off your old bad karma. Once you start hunting for faults, you never run out; so train the eye that finds the good instead.

**Frustration — nothing goes your way (v21).**
- *Cause:* You interrupted and disturbed others while they were doing something good — broke their focus, derailed their efforts.
- *Resolve:* Stop interrupting; instead encourage and rejoice in others' good work.
- *Example:* Pulling a focused coworker into idle chatter seeds your own frustration where nothing you attempt comes together. Protect others' focus, and yours protects itself.

**Everything falls into ruin (v31).**
- *Cause:* You treated cause and effect as if it didn't matter, and freely did harm — hurting, taking, deceiving.
- *Resolve:* Respect the law that acts have consequences, and act with patience and strength.
- *Example:* You sow for a good harvest and don't get even the seed back, while others succeed with ease — a sign of a deeper cause. The fix isn't more effort; it's cleaning up the actions that planted the ruin.

**No control; wandering with no home (v35).**
- *Cause:* You forced others out of their homes or places — even made your own parents or teachers leave.
- *Resolve:* Never displace people cruelly; instead help others find shelter.
- *Example:* Pushing someone out of their spot for your convenience seeds a restless, homeless life of your own. Help people find a place, and a place will hold for you.

**Work hit by disaster — drought, hail, pests (v36).**
- *Cause:* You failed to keep your moral commitments and the words of honor you gave. "Holes" in your morality ripen as disasters in your environment.
- *Resolve:* Keep your vows and commitments as cleanly as you can.
- *Example:* Repeated calamity that wrecks your harvest isn't just bad weather; it's leaked integrity showing up in the world around you. Seal the leaks — keep your word — and the ground steadies.

### Speech and relationships

**Foul, abusive words all around you (v14).**
- *Cause:* You slandered people, split friends apart, and hurled harsh, belittling words, running others down.
- *Resolve:* Guard your speech, keep it moderate, and stay humble.
- *Example:* The book notes that a jealous, low-merit person who constantly belittles others ruins no one's name but his own. Every belittling remark you hold back now is a harsh word you won't have to hear later.

**Losing dear friends (v16).**
- *Cause:* You took away others' friends and helpers, wanting them for yourself.
- *Resolve:* Never come between people who are close.
- *Example:* Luring away a friend's trusted helper because you wanted them seeds your own painful separations. Guard other people's bonds and your own will hold.

**Falsely blamed for what you didn't do (v18).**
- *Cause:* You despised and belittled your teachers and betters, and falsely accused others.
- *Resolve:* Never accuse anyone falsely; give people full credit for what they truly are.
- *Example:* The book tells of a monk jailed seven months for a theft he didn't commit — his drying robe stained, a calf gone missing nearby. Lifetimes back he had falsely accused a monk who was jailed seven days. The false charge came back multiplied.

**Everyone eager to blame only you (v23).**
- *Cause:* You were shameless — careless of others, thinking your deeds didn't matter at all.
- *Resolve:* Recover a sense of shame and decency (remember your acts are done, in effect, before the wise); stop the offensive behavior.
- *Example:* Acting as if no one's watching and nothing you do counts seeds a world where every finger points at you. Act as if it all matters — because it does.

**Friends and staff can't stand your habits (v24).**
- *Cause:* You forced your bad habits on others and were careless about how your behavior hit them.
- *Resolve:* Change; be gentle and kind, aware of your effect on people.
- *Example:* Little inconsiderate habits, pushed onto everyone around you, seed a home and office no one wants to stay in. Soften the habits, and people stay.

**Those closest turn into enemies (v25).**
- *Cause:* You held grudges, schemed quietly to harm, and faked affection while hiding base aims.
- *Resolve:* Drop pretense and hidden motives; never fake kindness while harboring harm.
- *Example:* The friend who turns on you is the return of an old grudge of yours. So when you feel the urge to nurse resentment or put on false warmth, drop it now — that's the very seed.

**Even well-meant acts meet hostility (v45).**
- *Cause:* You repaid kindness with malice — even held grudges against kind teachers who corrected you only out of care.
- *Resolve:* Accept others' kindness, and even their correction, humbly and gratefully.
- *Example:* The book's framing: every being has, at some time, been like a mother to you; forgetting that, you were unkind, and now the coldness returns. So even when scolded, honor the one who cares enough to correct you.

### Mentors, sincerity, and practice

**Good guides and mentors are displeased with you (v17).**
- *Cause:* You turned away from good guides and teachings and followed misleading friends instead.
- *Resolve:* Keep close to those who lift you up; gently step back from those who pull you off the path (without losing compassion for them).
- *Example:* Choosing the flattering, easy crowd over the ones who tell you the truth seeds a life where the good ones drift away from you. Stay near those who raise you.

**Nothing you do ever pleases your mentors (v22).**
- *Cause:* You were two-faced — pious and obedient in front of your teacher, but "like a dog or pig" behind their back.
- *Resolve:* Drop the hypocrisy; take the guidance sincerely to heart, in private as in public.
- *Example:* Nodding yes to advice, then doing the opposite the moment they're gone, seeds a relationship where nothing you do can satisfy them. Be the same person front and back.

**Unhappy; bored by good, drawn to bad (v20).**
- *Cause:* You led others into wrongdoing — gave the nudge or the setting that roused their worst side.
- *Resolve:* Never set people up to follow their negative traits.
- *Example:* Egging a coworker into a shady shortcut seeds your own dullness toward good and pull toward bad. Stop being the one who tempts, and your own pull toward good returns.

**Your mind clouds whenever you study (v28).**
- *Cause:* You treated learning as unimportant — whenever something had to be dropped from your life, wisdom was the first to go.
- *Resolve:* Listen, learn, and reflect now; you don't need to know everything — just what leads to freedom.
- *Example:* The book's image: you don't have to drink a whole stream to quench your thirst. Value learning enough to keep it in your life, and the fog over your mind lifts.

**Dull and sleepy during good work, wide awake for waste (v29).**
- *Cause:* You disrespected knowledge and looked down on the wise — even physically disrespected books, sitting on them or leaving them on the floor.
- *Resolve:* Show real respect for learning and endure some hardship for it.
- *Example:* The Kadampa lamas, even when very old, would stand up when texts were brought out. The more respect you show, the more respect you receive — and the more alert your mind grows for what matters.

**Your mind runs wild toward distraction (v30).**
- *Cause:* You never truly reflected that everything passes — that you will die — so you chase pleasures and busy-work instead of what matters.
- *Resolve:* Keep death and impermanence in view; it steadies and focuses the mind at every level.
- *Example:* The book tells of a monk lazy all his life who wept when old age suddenly arrived — he never expected it. Forget impermanence in the morning and the morning is wasted; remember it, and the wandering mind comes home.

**Your efforts bear no fruit (v32).**
- *Cause:* You leaned on shortcuts and false sources of help instead of clean action, and kept sliding toward the negative.
- *Resolve:* Leave the wrongdoing behind and stop looking to false fixes.
- *Example:* When even careful efforts to fix things only make them worse, don't blame the method — it's the pile of negative acts behind it. Clear those, and your efforts start to land.

**Prayers and wishes go unanswered (v33).**
- *Cause:* Your trust was weak — you half-believed your ideals, held distorted views, and never truly relied on what deserves reliance.
- *Resolve:* Whatever comes, place your real trust wholeheartedly in what's worthy — your teacher, the teaching, and good companions.
- *Example:* Praying hard while inwardly doubting is like sowing with one hand and pulling up the seed with the other. Commit fully, and the wishes finally find room to grow.

### The ego — "me first"

**Attachment and anger you can't suppress (v39).**
- *Cause:* However many antidotes you apply, they only grow — because you let self-cherishing ("me first") run wild for so long.
- *Resolve:* Uproot self-interest itself. This is the great enemy behind all the rest.
- *Example:* You try to calm a craving or a temper and it just swells. The book's point: you're treating symptoms. Turn on the real cause — "me first" — and the grip loosens.

**No progress no matter how you practice (v40).**
- *Cause:* Your motives were low and self-first, and you set aside genuinely good intentions.
- *Resolve:* Let every good thing you do be driven not by a fraction of selfishness, but purely by the wish to help others.
- *Example:* Doing good works mainly to look good or get ahead seeds a kind of stuckness. Rededicate the same actions to others' benefit, and they start to move you forward.

**Good deeds leave your mind untouched (v41).**
- *Cause:* You only ever did good by accident or for this life's gains, too fascinated by the material payoff.
- *Resolve:* Aim single-pointedly past quick wins toward lasting freedom.
- *Example:* Chasing only this-life results — money, status — leaves the heart unchanged however busy you are. Lift the aim higher, and the same efforts finally change you.

**Regret and doubt after doing good (v42).**
- *Cause:* You were fickle — keen at the start, quickly tired, badmouthing your own efforts — and courted only the powerful and rich.
- *Resolve:* Choose your commitments (and friends) with care, and see them through with self-awareness.
- *Example:* The book warns against the hunter who chases a hawk, then a fox, then a deer, and goes home empty-handed — like switching your practice every few months. Pick one worthwhile path and stay, and the regret disappears.

**Trusted friends scheme against you (v43).**
- *Cause:* You were egotistical and arrogant — thinking yourself the cleverest and best — and kept greedily taking from others.
- *Resolve:* Lower your pride, take less, and cultivate contentment.
- *Example:* The people who lure you with schemes are the return of your own arrogance and grasping. Meet the world with humility and contentment, and the schemers fall away.

**Likes and dislikes color everything you hear and say (v44).**
- *Cause:* You ignored the real source of your troubles — the delusion in your own heart — and even fed your self-cherishing (envy of a teacher, jealousy of what others learn).
- *Resolve:* Watch your delusions as they arise, examine them, and shake off their pull.
- *Example:* If even good teaching only stirs your envy, the problem isn't the teaching — it's the delusion filtering it. Name the delusion as it rises, and its grip on your ears and tongue weakens.

**In short (v46–48):** when unwanted pain "crashes down like thunder," it's your own arrow returning — you can never reap the results of someone else's actions, only your own. Blame no one else; do the Giving-and-Taking practice; and from now on carefully avoid every harmful act. As the Kadampa masters said, mind-training is the treasure behind every attainment: train it, and when harm comes you can stay steady — knowing suffering is simply the nature of an untrained cycle, and the way out is in your own hands.

## PART 5 — THE ONE ROOT: "ME FIRST"
Both books push past single faults to their single source.

- **The real enemy is two things:** *self-grasping* (believing in a solid, separate "I") and *self-cherishing* (putting your own welfare first). One is the boss; the other is his helper. *"All that is wrong can be traced to one source: our concern for ourselves."*
- **The mirror (Wheel v49–91):** a long, honest list of how "me first" shows up — hypocrisy, hoarding, boasting, grudges, flattering the powerful, fine talk with poor conduct, blaming others for our own faults. Read it as a mirror for *yourself*, not a list of others' sins. Each verse ends: *"Trample the self-centered butcher who slaughters our chance for freedom."*
- **The freeing truth:** the "I" you defend so hard was never solid to begin with. All things are like a face in water or a torch whirled into a ring of fire — real enough to matter, but not fixed. So here's where both books meet: **things aren't fixed (you can change them) + cause and effect never fails (seeds always grow) = you can design your future by choosing your seeds.** The practical takeaway they repeat: *in this world of appearances, always do good and avoid what brings pain.*

## PART 6 — THE ACT OF TRUTH & THE NIGHTLY LOG
*(The practical engine of the whole system — Diamond Cutter, Chapter 8.)*

### Why good people sometimes seem unrewarded
1. **Causes come before results.** Someone doing well now is enjoying *past* generosity — and might be planting ruin *now* by being greedy. (An apple pie means a tree already grew, not one growing under the floor.)
2. **Causes are smaller than results.** A small kindness, grown over time, becomes great wealth.
3. **Growing takes time.** You don't stand in the garden the day after planting, angry no flowers came up. People "fail" with this for just two reasons: they quit too soon, or they do it loosely while thinking they do it well.
4. **You need to track.** The old monks carried black and white pebbles — a white one to the pocket for each kind act, a black for each unkind one, counted at night. At first the black far outnumber the white. But the mind is very trainable.
5. **Understanding makes it far stronger.** Being good by habit or fear is weak. Being good while you *know what seed it plants* is powerful.
6. **End with the Act of Truth.**

### The nightly log (once a day)
> "One of the most important things in this book."

**Set up:** From the 46 fixes, pick your **top 1–3 problems**. As one improves, swap in the next. Keep a small notebook (or use the app).

**Each night, for each chosen problem, write:**
- **➕ a win** — the best thing you thought/said/did on it today. Be *exact*: not "I was nice," but *"3:15 — thanked Susan at her desk for the inventories."*
- **➖ a slip** — one thing you did poorly on it, equally exact.
- **✔ a to-do** — one small, easy, symbolic step for tomorrow.

Then write the day's **best three** and **worst three** thoughts, words, or deeds.

**The right attitude:** This is **not** about guilt — there's no word for "guilty" here, only *"smart regret that decides to do things differently."* It's calm gardening: choosing which seeds to plant, then enjoying the harvest. **By tracking, you change; by changing, your world changes.** Keep it specific, keep it short, keep it up.

### The Act of Truth (how to end the day)
On a day of real (or near-real) honesty and kindness, say this — and picture warm golden light from your heart flowing to everyone near you, then everyone heading home, then everyone waiting for them:

> *If it's true that today I was honest and kind in all I did, said, and even thought toward others — then may a new power be born. And by it, may I and everyone in my world find real happiness and success, together.*

Calling on the truth of what you actually did lifts the whole day's seeds to a new level of strength.

## PART 7 — THE CORE PRACTICES

### A. Morning Silent Time (set the day)
15–30 minutes, **same time every day** so it becomes a habit like eating.
1. **The night before:** in bed, review the day (best three / worst three), rest on the good, and plan to wake well.
2. **Don't do it in bed** (you'll fall asleep). Get up, freshen up.
3. **A clean, quiet corner** — phone off, no interruptions. Tidy it first (this stands for tidying your life).
4. **Sit up straight**, relaxed, hands on your lap, eyes soft or closed.
5. **Settle the breath:** watch the air at your nose — out-breath first, then in. Count ten breaths. Lose count? Start over.
6. **Work one problem:** recall a real recent moment of it → see it wasn't fixed (others didn't see it as a problem) → trace it to your own past seed → rehearse the *right* response for when it comes again (answer coldness with warmth, insult with none).
7. **Finish** by picturing your ideal self — generous, healthy, honest, wise. Then go.

**Example:** your problem is "no one helps me in a crunch." In the quiet, you replay a recent time you were refused help, see that it grew from a seed you planted by once refusing someone, then rehearse: next time I'm refused, I'll *still* help them back. A day or two later it happens for real — and because you practiced, the kind response comes automatically.

### B. Giving and Taking — *tonglen*
Use it with each Wheel line, or whenever pain hits:
- Picture a wise, loving being before you. Ask: *"May the suffering of all beings ripen on me."*
- **Breathe in** others' pain as black smoke; it dissolves your "me first" at your heart.
- **Breathe out** your happiness and calm as light to everyone.
- Especially welcome your *own* pain — hunger, illness, insult — as a chance to take on others' pain and burn your self-centeredness. *"Like peacocks eating poison and thriving."*

### C. Gratitude
Sit 5–10 minutes. Walk through the good in your life and the people behind each part — your teacher, your family, the delivery person, the countless hands behind your breakfast. Paid or not, they gave hours of a short life to help you. Happy people feel this keenly; unhappy people avoid the thought. Thank people, often.

### D. Guard your speech
A huge share of these problems come from talk. Speak only with purpose. Cut gossip, harsh words, and news you can't act on and won't even remember (try the three-day test: what do you recall from that newspaper?). Silence is powerful and practical.

## PART 8 — HOW TO LIVE IT, DAY BY DAY

**The night before:** review the day (best three / worst three); plan to wake well.

**Morning:** rise, tidy your quiet corner, do Silent Time (breaths → work one problem → picture your ideal self). Add a little tonglen and gratitude. Glance at your top problems.

**Through the day — plant seeds on purpose:**
- Keep a generous, open mind — never a cheap one, even when money's tight.
- Be fully honest in every word; not the smallest lie or fudge.
- No harsh talk, no dividing talk, no idle chatter. Speak with purpose.
- No anger — and step back from its prelude before it forms.
- Wish everyone well, rivals included; feel joy at others' wins, never at their troubles.
- Stay humble; give credit away; be grateful.
- Help others' work; keep every promise; respect their space and health.
- Break every cycle of conflict yourself — answer coldness with warmth.
- Watch for "me first" as it rises, and turn it toward helping someone.

**At night (once):** write your log — ➕ / ➖ / ✔ for your top problems, plus best three / worst three. No guilt, just specifics. Then say the Act of Truth and picture the golden light. Then sleep.

**Over months:** keep it up like learning piano — daily, patiently, before judging results. Re-read this once a week. Don't blindly trust inherited ideas about where success comes from. Results have causes; repeat the causes, get the results.

---

### The whole thing in one line
> Your world — money, health, people, even your own mind — is the fruit of seeds you planted by how you treated others. Nothing is fixed; every seed grows. So watch what you do, say, and think; track it once a night without guilt; set each morning in silence; take on others' pain and give them your joy; drop "me first"; and seal each honest day with an Act of Truth. Do this steadily, knowing *why*, and you design your own future.
